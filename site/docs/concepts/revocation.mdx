---
title: Revocation
description: Signed snapshots, a sequence that only moves forward, and why that matters.
---

A verifier is handed the revocation list. It never fetches one, so your app
decides how fresh the list is and can say so.

## Signing a snapshot

```swift
let snapshot = try Mark.signSnapshot(seed: rootSeed, seq: 42, issuedAt: now, ids: ids)
```

```c
GmStatus gm_snapshot_sign(const uint8_t *seed, size_t seed_len,
                          uint64_t seq, int64_t issued_at,
                          const uint8_t *ids, size_t ids_len,
                          GmBuffer *out);
```

Ids are a flat run of 16-byte values.

## Opening one

```swift
let (ids, seq, issuedAt) = try Mark.openSnapshot(snapshot, roots: [rootKey], heldSeq: held)
```

```c
GmStatus gm_snapshot_open(const uint8_t *snapshot, size_t snapshot_len,
                          const uint8_t *roots, size_t roots_len,
                          uint64_t held_seq,
                          GmBuffer *out_ids, uint64_t *out_seq, int64_t *out_issued_at);
```

It checks the snapshot against the roots and, when it's newer than the
sequence you hold, writes the ids out.

## Why an older sequence is refused

An older or equal sequence answers `GM_STALE`.

Without that rule, anyone able to serve your client an old file could bring a
revoked badge back by replaying it. The sequence only moves forward, so a
replay is a no-op rather than an attack.

Keep the sequence you hold alongside the ids, and pass it every time.

## How fresh is fresh enough

`out_issued_at` tells you when the snapshot was made. That's your signal, and
it's a product decision what to do with it: show the badge with a note,
refuse to show it, or quietly refresh.

The library hands you the number and makes no choice for you.
